PSSSB (Punjab Subordinate Services Selection Board) publishes its own typing-test instruction sheets for specific posts, and for Clerk-category recruitment the figures are unusually well-documented: candidates need a minimum gross speed of 30 words per minute in both Punjabi and English, with a maximum of 8% mistakes permissible. Both languages are required, not an either/or choice — a real distinction from exams where you pick one language, so Punjab clerk preparation needs genuine practice in both Punjabi and English rather than concentrating on whichever feels more comfortable. Punjabi typing on PSSSB tests uses the Raavi font, a Unicode-compliant typeface, which is worth confirming you've practiced specifically — Raavi's key mappings aren't identical to generic Gurmukhi typing tools that may use a different font or layout, so test on Raavi specifically before your actual exam rather than assuming any Punjabi typing practice transfers directly. PSSSB's own instruction sheet reports gross speed rather than net, with the 8% mistake allowance functioning as the accuracy check rather than a per-error deduction from your speed score the way some other exams calculate net WPM — meaning as long as your error rate stays under 8% of total characters, your gross typing speed is what's compared against the 30 WPM minimum, not a reduced net figure. This makes PSSSB's scoring somewhat more forgiving of occasional mistakes than a strict net-WPM system, provided your overall error rate stays well under that 8% ceiling. The test is described as qualifying in nature across the sources I found — a pass/fail outcome rather than a scored component feeding into your final merit, so once you're reliably clearing 30 WPM gross with comfortably under 8% errors in both languages, additional speed work provides diminishing returns for this specific test, though it never hurts for general competence. A worked check: typing 320 characters in a minute-long sample gives roughly 64 gross words per minute at five characters per word, with an error rate of 4% — well within the 8% ceiling and double the 30 WPM minimum, illustrating that most people who've built solid touch-typing fundamentals in either language clear PSSSB's bar with real margin once they're specifically comfortable in Raavi for Punjabi. A mistake worth avoiding specifically for PSSSB is treating Punjabi and English as a single combined requirement rather than two separately required 30 WPM tests — since PSSSB's instruction sheet requires both languages, not either/or, practicing heavily in one at the expense of the other means you'll likely fail the component you neglected regardless of how strong your other language is.
