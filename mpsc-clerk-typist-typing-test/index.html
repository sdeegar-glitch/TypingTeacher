MPSC Clerk-Typist's typing requirement has one genuine point of confusion worth resolving before you prepare: sources disagree on whether Marathi and English are both required, or whether clearing either one is sufficient. Some recent listings state the requirement as Marathi 30 WPM OR English 40 WPM — meaning passing just one clears the typing component — while other guides and older notices describe both as separately required tests you must qualify in independently. Given this genuinely conflicts across sources, confirm the AND/OR rule from your own MPSC Group C advertisement before deciding whether to concentrate on one language or split your practice evenly across both. Keystroke targets reported alongside the WPM figures are 2,000 keystrokes in English and 1,500 keystrokes in Marathi, each within a 10-minute window per some sources, which converts to roughly 40 WPM English and 30 WPM Marathi at five characters per word — consistent with the WPM figures above, giving some confidence these numbers pair correctly even without an official source confirming both directly. Keyboard layout for Marathi is the other genuinely unsettled detail: one source describes a Remington Marathi keyboard (a Unicode-based font, despite the older Remington name), with a 300-word passage over 10 minutes; another source says the standard layout for modern MPSC exams is Mangal INSCRIPT, while noting some older departmental exams still use KrutiDev 055. These are three different layout families (Remington, Mangal/INSCRIPT and Kruti Dev), and typing habits don't transfer cleanly between any of them — this is one of the clearest cases on this site where guessing wrong wastes real preparation time, so if your MPSC notification doesn't state the layout plainly, it's worth checking directly with MPSC or practicing a baseline level of comfort across more than one layout. The test is qualifying in nature — no marks are awarded for the typing test itself, so like most qualifying tests covered here, the practical goal is reliably clearing whichever language requirement (or both) your specific notification sets, not maximizing speed once you're comfortably above it. A worked example at the English figure: typing 2,050 keystrokes in 10 minutes with 15 uncorrected errors gives gross words = 2,050 ÷ 5 = 410, net words = 410 − 15 = 395, net WPM = 39.5 — just under the commonly cited 40 WPM target, illustrating how closely error count erodes an otherwise-adequate raw typing pace. A mistake specific to MPSC Clerk-Typist is practicing only in whichever language feels easier without first confirming the AND/OR rule — if your actual notification requires both languages independently rather than either one, concentrating on just your stronger language leaves you unprepared for a requirement you didn't know existed until it was too late to build the other language's speed in time.
