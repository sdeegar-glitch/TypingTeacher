The SSC CGL Data Entry Skill Test (DEST) applies specifically to Tax Assistant posts in CBIC and CBDT, and is a different test from the typing tests used for SSC CHSL or other CGL posts — SSC's own instructions describe it as a skill test in data entry, administered for a duration of 15 minutes on passages containing text of 2,000 key depressions, which works out to roughly 8,000 key depressions per hour, the figure most guides quote. If you're applying for a CGL post that doesn't require data entry, you won't take this test at all; it's worth confirming from your own post preference whether DEST applies to you before building a practice routine around it. DEST measures key depressions, not words per minute the way CHSL's typing test does — every character you type, including spaces and punctuation, counts as one key depression, so 2,000 key depressions in 15 minutes works out to roughly 133 per minute, or about 27 WPM at five characters per word. SSC's own instructions confirm the test is qualifying only — it doesn't add to your merit score, but you must meet the speed to be selected for data entry-track Tax Assistant posts. Candidates who've opted for both the Computer Proficiency Test and the Skill Test in Data Entry only take the Proficiency Test, not DEST separately, so check which track your specific post falls under. Accuracy requirements are one area where sources genuinely disagree: some guides recommend 95% as a safe target, others cite 80% as the actual cutoff, and neither figure appears explicitly in the SSC instructions I could verify. Given that uncertainty, the safer approach is to treat 95%+ as your practice target regardless of which cutoff turns out to be correct — the cost of extra accuracy practice is low, while the cost of discovering the real cutoff is higher than you assumed is a failed skill test. A worked example: at 133 key depressions per minute sustained for 15 minutes, that's 1,995 keystrokes — just under the 2,000 target, so most candidates should practice toward comfortably exceeding 133 per minute rather than hitting it exactly, since any slowdown from corrections or hesitation eats directly into your margin. Each backspace and retype costs you keystrokes without adding to your depression count, so accuracy under sustained typing — not burst speed — is what actually determines whether you clear 2,000 in the full 15 minutes. Candidates who prepare only on prose passages sometimes struggle more than expected on DEST, because the actual passage content is still natural-language text rather than numeric data — unlike a pure data-entry role, DEST measures key depressions on readable sentences, so prose typing practice does transfer here, just measured differently (key depressions rather than words). A more common mistake is misjudging the pass margin: since 2,000 key depressions in 15 minutes is a modest pace for anyone with reasonable touch-typing fundamentals, candidates sometimes under-practice DEST relative to CHSL's typing test, assuming the lower-sounding "key depression" framing means an easier bar — in practice the two tests require comparable underlying typing ability once you convert both to the same units. Treat DEST with the same seriousness as any other qualifying skill test: a single missed cutoff removes you from selection for that post regardless of your written-exam score.
