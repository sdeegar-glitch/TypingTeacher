UPSSSC (Uttar Pradesh Subordinate Services Selection Commission) sets 25 WPM Hindi and 30 WPM English as the most consistently reported typing-test requirement for Junior Assistant and Computer Operator posts, tied to a specific advertisement number each recruitment cycle rather than a single standing rule — different cycles have run under different advertisement numbers (12/Exam/2024 and 08/Pariksha/2023 have both been cited), so the exact figure and format you need to prepare for depends on which advertisement your application falls under. Font and layout options reported for UPSSSC are more flexible than most exams covered here: candidates may use either Mangal (typed in the Inscript layout) or Kruti Dev 010 (typed in the older Remington/typewriter layout), giving you a genuine choice rather than one mandated font — if you already have a strong Hindi layout from other exam preparation, UPSSSC's test has reportedly accepted either, though you should confirm this is still the case for your specific advertisement before assuming it. Test duration is one area where sources disagree outright: some UPSSSC notices and news reports describe a short 5-minute test for each language, while other practice tools and guides list a 10-minute duration. This is a meaningful difference to resolve before you practice, since pacing for a 5-minute sprint is different from pacing for a 10-minute sustained test — check your specific admit card for the stated duration rather than assuming either figure. Error penalties also vary across cited sources: one describes 5 errors as admissible before a penalty of 5 words is deducted per additional error, while another source describes a flat 10% error limit — these are different systems (a graduated penalty versus a hard ceiling) and shouldn't be averaged together. Practicing toward very low error rates (under 2-3%) sidesteps the ambiguity entirely, since you'd clear either version of the rule comfortably. A worked example assuming the 5-minute format: typing 150 Hindi characters per minute for 5 minutes gives 750 characters, 150 gross words at five characters per word, and with 3 uncorrected errors nets (150 − 3) ÷ 5 = 29.4 WPM — well above the 25 WPM Hindi floor even in a compressed window, which is worth knowing since a shorter test leaves less room to recover from a slow start. Since UPSSSC runs recruitment cycles under different advertisement numbers with potentially different rules each time, the most common mistake is reusing guidance written for a previous cycle's advertisement without checking whether the current one matches — duration, error rules and even font availability have reportedly varied between cycles, so a guide (including this one) written without reference to a specific current advertisement number should be treated as a general starting point, not a substitute for your own admit card's stated rules.
