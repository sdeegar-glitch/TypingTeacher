Lower Division Clerk (LDC) is a post title used by dozens of different recruiting bodies — SSC, DSSSB, RSMSSB, various state subordinate service boards and more — and each sets its own typing-test speed, so "the LDC typing test" covers a real range rather than one fixed number. At the higher end, SSC CHSL's LDC/JSA typing test requires 35 WPM English or 30 WPM Hindi over 10 minutes. DSSSB's LDC posts have most commonly been reported at a similar 35/30 on computer, though older DSSSB circulars used a lower 30 WPM English / 25 WPM Hindi, so even within one recruiting body the figure has shifted across cycles. At the lower end, several state LDC posts — RSMSSB's among them — report figures closer to 25 WPM Hindi / 30 WPM English, and some older or smaller-state LDC notices go as low as 20 WPM. What stays consistent across nearly every LDC typing test is that it's qualifying, not merit-scored — you pass or you don't, and the typing result generally doesn't add to the marks that determine your final rank. Net WPM, not gross, is almost universally the scoring method: total characters divided by five, minus your error count, divided by minutes, which means a careful 32 WPM gross with almost no mistakes nets higher than a rushed 38 WPM gross riddled with corrections. Language choice also varies by recruiting body: SSC CHSL requires you to pick English or Hindi on your application and commit to it, while some DSSSB and state LDC posts allow either language at the test itself. If you're preparing for a specific LDC recruitment, the single most useful thing to confirm early is which exact body is running it — SSC, DSSSB, or a state board — since that determines both the speed requirement and whether you've already committed to a language. A practical preparation target that clears nearly every LDC variant: 38+ WPM in English and 33+ WPM in Hindi, built with consistent, low-error practice rather than occasional fast bursts. That comfortably exceeds the highest common LDC figure (35/30) with margin, while still being achievable within a few weeks of focused daily practice for most typists starting from a baseline of 20-25 WPM. The most common mistake in LDC preparation is treating it as one exam rather than a post title used by many different recruiting bodies: a candidate who prepared only for SSC CHSL's 35/30 figure and then applies to a state LDC post with a different speed, font or language rule can be caught off guard by rules they never checked because they assumed "LDC" meant the same test everywhere. Before committing practice time, identify your specific recruiting body and post code, and read that body's own typing-test instructions rather than a generic LDC guide, including this one.
