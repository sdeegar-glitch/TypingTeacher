Bihar SSC (BSSC) and the Bihar Police Subordinate Services Commission (BPSSC) both run typing and stenography skill tests for different posts, and the exact rules vary more between these recruitment bodies and posts than for most other state exams covered here — there's no single authoritative BSSC typing notice that covers every post, so treat any figure, including the ones below, as a starting point to verify against your specific post's notification. The most consistently reported figure is 30 WPM in Hindi, tested over 10 minutes, roughly matching 300 words in that window. Where sources genuinely disagree is the error tolerance: one guide reports an error margin as low as 1.5% for certain stenographer-track tests, another cites 5% for a general Bihar typing test, and a 2026 Bihar Police ASI Steno guide lists up to 10% for the English component specifically. Given this spread, the safest practice target is well under any of these ceilings — aim for well under 1% errors in practice so that whichever cutoff your specific post actually uses, you clear it with margin rather than discovering the stricter figure applied to you. Font requirements are similarly unsettled across sources: some say Kruti Dev remains accepted for Bihar exams, while a Bihar Police-specific guide describes BPSSC using Mangal with Inscript layout or Remington Gail (effectively Kruti Dev) depending on the specific recruitment, and explicitly tells candidates to check their admit card for the mandated layout. This is one of the clearest cases on this site where guessing the wrong layout wastes real preparation time — if your admit card doesn't state the font plainly, it's worth asking the recruiting body directly or practicing a baseline level of comfort in both Kruti Dev and Mangal rather than committing fully to one. A worked example using the most common figures: typing 310 characters per minute sustained for 10 minutes gives 3,100 characters, or 620 gross words at five characters per word — far above what any BSSC notice requires, which tells you the real constraint for most candidates isn't raw speed but accuracy and layout familiarity.
