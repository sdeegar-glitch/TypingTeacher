RRB Typing Test — Railway Clerk cum Typist Practice

Free RRB typing test practice for Railway Junior/Senior Clerk cum Typist and Typist posts — 30 WPM English / 25 WPM Hindi, with live WPM and accuracy.

RRB (Railway Recruitment Board) typing tests apply specifically to posts with "cum Typist" in the title — Junior Clerk cum Typist, Senior Clerk cum Typist, Accounts Clerk cum Typist among them — not to every railway recruitment; posts like Station Master, Traffic Assistant and Goods Guard carry no typing component at all. A Railway Recruitment notice states the requirement plainly: candidates are required to pass the typewriting test at the minimum speed of 30 words per minute in English and 25 wpm in Hindi on Personal Computer, over a 10-minute test.

One source describes this as a net-speed figure with a specific error penalty — each error reduces 0.5 WPM from your gross speed — meaning a candidate typing 32 WPM gross with 4 errors would net 32 − (4 × 0.5) = 30 WPM, exactly at the qualifying line. Accuracy requirements reported alongside this vary meaningfully by source, from 85-90% up to 95%, so rather than targeting a specific percentage that may not match your actual notice, the more reliable approach is minimizing errors outright — at the 0.5-WPM-per-error penalty rate, even a handful of mistakes meaningfully erodes your margin above 30.

The test is qualifying only — passing it is necessary for selection to a cum-Typist post, but your score doesn't add to the merit ranking that was already decided by your written exam. That means the practical goal is consistency at 30/25 under test conditions, not maximizing speed once you're reliably clearing it; time spent building to 45+ WPM provides little additional benefit if 35 WPM reliably clears the bar with margin.

A worked example using the stated penalty rule: typing 33 WPM gross with 2 errors nets 33 − (2 × 0.5) = 32 WPM — comfortably above 30. The same candidate rushing to 38 WPM gross but with 10 errors nets 38 − 5 = 33, barely better despite typing noticeably faster and far less reliably from one attempt to the next — illustrating why controlled, accurate typing beats maximum-effort fast typing for a test scored this way.

A common mistake is confusing this general "cum Typist" requirement with the separate, differently-figured RRB NTPC Typing Skill Test covered elsewhere on this site — both are RRB typing tests, but they apply to different post categories and in some reported cases use slightly different accuracy conventions, so check which specific post and notification you're preparing for rather than assuming all RRB typing requirements are identical.

Accuracy thresholds and some post eligibility details vary between RRB zones and recruitment notices, so your specific zone's current requirement, not a general figure, should guide your prep.
